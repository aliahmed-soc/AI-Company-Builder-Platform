# ADR-002 — Infrastructure Ownership Model

1. **Title:** Infrastructure ownership and provisioning model
2. **Status:** Accepted
3. **Date:** 2026-07-18
4. **Decision owner:** Product owner
5. **Context:** When software generation ships (post-MVP: BUILD/DEPLOY), generated projects need repositories, databases, hosting, and domains. Whether the platform or the customer owns that infrastructure shapes tenancy, credential, and resource-lifecycle foundations that must be poured during the architecture phase. Three models were analyzed in OWNER-DECISION-PACK.md (Decision 2) against 12 criteria. This is the highest-reversal-cost decision in the pack.
6. **Decision:** **Hybrid long-term product direction, with platform-managed defaults implemented first.** For MVP: the platform manages the default execution environment and may manage generated-project hosting and related services; users are never required to configure hosting, databases, or developer infrastructure during onboarding; generated source code, generated documents, and customer-owned data must be exportable; the architecture should avoid unnecessary obstacles to future transfer; provider abstractions are used where they provide practical value; **full infrastructure portability is not an MVP promise.** Marketing must not claim: one-click cloud migration, complete database migration, automatic account transfer, provider-independent deployments, or guaranteed production portability. Long-term: advanced customers may connect customer-owned infrastructure; generated projects may be transferred out; portability expands incrementally; source-code and data ownership remain core principles.
7. **Alternatives considered:** Fully platform-managed forever (simplest, strongest lock-in narrative, fails technical/agency segments long-term); customer-owned infrastructure only (best portability, catastrophic onboarding friction for the ADR-001 segment, heterogeneous support burden). Full tables preserved in the decision pack.
8. **Reasons:** Managed-first matches directly observed reference-product behavior and the ADR-001 segment's abilities; hybrid sequencing captures managed simplicity now without foreclosing ownership principles later; MVP-era cost is near zero because generation itself is post-MVP.
9. **Positive consequences:** Zero-infrastructure onboarding; full platform control of quality gates, rollback, and health; single stack to support; clear security responsibility.
10. **Negative consequences:** Platform carries hosting cost and abuse surface; lock-in perception must be actively managed; transfer tooling is deferred debt.
11. **Risks:** **Recorded tension:** a temporary, real gap exists between platform-managed defaults and complete portability — at the point generation ships, customers will have *export* but not *transfer*. Also: orphaned-resource cost leaks; hosted-content abuse/compliance exposure; marketing overreach promising portability that does not exist.
12. **Mitigations:** Export commitments are binding (BUILD-003 parity; EXPORT-001 at beta); artifact/manifest formats designed transfer-aware (EXPORT-002 direction); explicit marketing prohibitions listed in the decision; resource-lifecycle management and cost attribution designed in from the start; D-07 owns the public promise wording.
13. **Reversal cost:** **High** — tenancy, credential, and resource-lifecycle foundations follow from this choice; flipping to customer-owned-primary later is a rebuild of the operational core.
14. **PRD requirement IDs affected:** BUILD-001, BUILD-002, BUILD-003, BUILD-004, BUILD-005, BUILD-006, BUILD-007, DEPLOY-001, DEPLOY-002, EXPORT-001, EXPORT-002, INTEG-001, INTEG-002, INTEG-003, NFR-001, NFR-014, NFR-017.
15. **Architecture areas affected:** Tenancy and resource-lifecycle model; credential vault; hosting/cost-attribution design; export/manifest formats; connector-framework boundaries (customer-owned connections are a later extension point, not an MVP path).
16. **Follow-up decisions:** Which managed infrastructure providers (architecture phase, per the provider-abstraction rule); D-07 ownership/export promise strength; transfer-tooling scope and timing (future ADR).
17. **Review trigger:** Software-generation phase entering design; a design partner requiring customer-owned infrastructure; managed-hosting unit economics failing the D-02 model.
